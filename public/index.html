Where this started

The answer was always “ask Ahmad.”

I had a question about something on an aircraft. My manager said, "Ask Ahmad*." My mentor, with decades in aerospace, said, "Ask Ahmad." So I asked what happens if Ahmad isn't there. Nobody had an answer. We sat on our hands, because the only copy of what we needed lived in one person's head. (*name changed)

I saw it again and again. We default to a who instead of a what, a person instead of a document, and the experience walks out the door with them.

You've probably lived this too. You needed an answer, and the one person who had it wasn't there. That's the problem I work on now: get it out of the head and onto paper, then let AI help.

How TIGER works

Nothing flies without a framework. Here's mine for AI.

I spent seventeen years in aerospace: quality assurance, FAA certification, and training technical teams on the 787 and A350 in multiple countries. A procedure only counts once it's written down, followed and checked. TIGER brings that discipline to working with AI.

The TIGER Framework™ workshop: a hands-on session that takes teams from "we should use AI" to working use cases they own, in a single sitting.

  1. T

    Task with context

    Say what the job is, and give the AI the background it needs to do it well.

  2. I

    Instruct with Intent

    Tell it exactly what to do, and what finished output you want back.

  3. G

    Guardrails

    Set the limits up front: what it must never do, say or sell. No trucks for $1.

  4. E

    Evaluate

    Check what comes back against what you know is true. You are the final check.

  5. R

    Refine

    Fix what is off and run it again, until the result is one you would stand behind.

AI as an assistant whose work gets checked, not a source of truth.

FAQ

How do I start documenting business processes?

Start small: pick the three tasks that would hurt most if you were out for two weeks. For each, write down what triggers it, the steps, who does what, and how you know it's done right. Then have someone else try to follow it and fix wherever they get stuck. Don't try to write the whole business down at once.

Can AI write SOPs?

It can help draft one from what you tell it, but it can't know what's only in your head, and it can swap in its own wording or get details wrong. Your review is the step that makes the SOP yours. That's why TIGER starts with the task and its context, and builds in Evaluate and Refine.

How can AI help me run my small business?

In bounded, routine tasks where you can check the result: reformatting information, first drafts you then edit, routine admin. Whether it fits your business depends on how your work actually runs, which is what the readiness assessment looks at. [Add one real example from your practice, if available]

Should nonprofits use AI?

It's a values and risk decision, and thoughtful people in the sector disagree. Some use it for routine tasks with strict rules; others refuse. The assessment helps you decide for your organization, including where AI is off-limits, and "no" is an acceptable result.

What should an AI policy for a nonprofit include?

Common elements: which tasks AI may and may not be used for, how donor and client data is protected, who reviews AI-assisted work, whether and how you disclose its use, who approves new tools, and how often the policy is revisited. This is general information, not legal advice; have your board or counsel review your final policy.
